Overview

BC Parks lists the Wedgemount Lake trail at 6 km from the parking lot to the lake with about 1,200 m of elevation gain, and rates it difficult and very steep (Type IV). The 2 km access road from Highway 99 is improved gravel and is not plowed in winter.

BC Parks asks hikers to follow the orange trail markers and the main trail, and to avoid trail braiding to protect the sensitive environment.

Getting there

The road

From Highway 99 a 2 km improved gravel road leads to the parking lot. BC Parks says it is not plowed in winter.

Camping

Reservations are required year-round for Wedge Campground, and all camping needs a permit.

Day trip

BC Parks says day visitors should be capable of camping overnight in case of injury, accident or extreme weather.

Rules & safety

Prohibited

BC Parks prohibits motorized vehicles, domestic animals, fires and drones in Garibaldi Provincial Park year-round.

Weather

BC Parks warns that Garibaldi's weather can be severe and unforgiving, and that day visitors should be capable of camping overnight in an emergency.

Pack it out

The park is user-maintained with no garbage removal. Use the outhouses provided, and bring your own toilet paper beyond trailheads.

Stay on trail

Follow the orange markers and main trail. Avoid braiding to protect fragile alpine vegetation.

Glacier and avalanche

BC Parks says avalanche and glacier hazards exist in Garibaldi year-round, and travelling beyond the maintained trail needs special skills and equipment.

The descent

1,200 m of descent is hard on knees and ankles and is where many trips run late: carry a headlamp.

Questions

How long is the Wedgemount Lake trail?

BC Parks lists 6 km from the parking lot to the lake with about 1,200 m of elevation gain. It rates the trail difficult and very steep.

What is the road like?

A 2 km improved gravel road from Highway 99. BC Parks says it is not plowed in winter.

Can I camp at the lake?

Yes, at Wedge Campground, with a camping permit and a reservation.

Can I bring my dog?

No. Domestic animals are prohibited in Garibaldi Provincial Park.

Safety checklist

Tell someone your plan

Leave your route, group size and expected return time with someone you trust, and check in when you are back. BC AdventureSmart (adventuresmart.ca) has a free trip-plan form.

Check conditions the same day

Look at the weather, road and trail conditions, closures and (in snow season) the avalanche forecast on the day you go, not last week. Use the land manager's page for this guide.

Set a turnaround time

Pick a time to turn back whatever happens, start early, and carry a headlamp. Most trips go wrong in the last hours of the day.

Carry the essentials

Water, food, navigation (map and offline GPS), light, first aid, extra layers, sun protection, a whistle and fire or shelter. A phone alone is not a plan.

Emergencies

Call 911. Cell coverage is unreliable or absent in much of the Sea to Sky backcountry, so consider a satellite messenger and know your location.

Water and wildlife

Wear a life jacket on the water: cold water can disable you in minutes. Carry bear spray where bears are present, keep food secured and give wildlife space.
